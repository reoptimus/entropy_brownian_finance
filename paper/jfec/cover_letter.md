Dear Editors,

Please find enclosed the manuscript "What Survives a Placebo? Entropy, Market Coupling and the Plausibility of Stress Scenarios", which I submit for consideration in the *Journal of Financial Econometrics*.

Entropy and correlation-concentration indicators of financial stress are increasingly used by researchers and supervisors. The paper shows that most of the crisis signatures such indicators display (jumps, slow relaxation, fattening tails, co-movement of tails and correlation) are produced by the rolling estimator itself and are reproduced, often exaggerated, by a resampling null with no dynamics. One fact survives a dynamics-free null, a volatility-preserving constant-correlation GARCH null and a within-year comparison, on US stocks, US industries and euro-area markets: under stress, correlations couple and the effective number of independent risk sources falls. The GARCH null also overturns a more appealing claim, that crises are dominated by dependence. The paper gives the finite-sample bias of the coupling estimator, a Monte Carlo study of the size and power of the test (the dynamics-free null over-rejects when volatility follows a GARCH process, the GARCH null over-rejects when volatility switches regimes, so the paper requires a result to pass both; in large cross-sections this joint rule also needs a shrunk correlation estimator), and an application that prices supervisory stress scenarios in nats, including a result on the cheapest covariance change that achieves a given portfolio severity and an audit of the EBA 2025 adverse scenario.

I believe the paper fits the journal's interest in the measurement of risk and dependence and in sound inference for financial data. The data and code that reproduce every table and figure are provided in the replication package, in line with the journal's data policy; the euro-area panel used in the online appendix is built from public ECB, FRED and Yahoo Finance data by a script included in the package.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. [If applicable: an earlier, policy-oriented version circulated as a preprint; give the reference.]
